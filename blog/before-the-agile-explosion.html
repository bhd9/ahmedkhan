Before the Agile Explosion

How I became an Agilist before I knew what Agile was

My journey to agile began almost accidentally with an unexpected career switch. However, my personal growth as an agilist has been as organic as a river shaping sedimentary layers—steady, natural, and strengthened by time.

Almost twenty years ago, I reluctantly agreed to join my friend's web development startup, leaving a network project manager job with decent benefits. The startup had a team of very sharp developers, all based in Bangalore, India. My job was to generate leads, close sales, collect requirements, coordinate with developers, do demos, and collect money, while sitting in a small office in Norcross, Georgia. I didn't have much experience in this business, so it was about learning iteratively—one feature, one insight at a time. Before I knew it, I was functioning as an agile Product Owner and a Scrum Master; long before I even knew those titles existed.

Although initial fear gave way to excitement as I mastered the work, the lack of adequate pay and benefits soon necessitated a move to a larger company. With three children under ten, this decision was simply a matter of responsibility. I got a contract QA position offer, at a well-known online vehicle-sales company, that I couldn't refuse. They were a pure waterfall company at the time, but such an organized, well-oiled software development machine. Not Agile by design but really fast with little to no waste.

A Mentor Who Changed Everything

I was challenged to build an end-to-end regression suite from scratch while still testing for regular releases. Their legendary QA manager (now retired), was an incredible mentor and coach; firm, insightful, and deeply committed to quality.

It was there that I learned the true meaning of hands-on work. I got closer to the process, began to understand the language, and started bridging the gap between IT and business. I also developed strong ties with the deployment and application support teams, learning how software truly comes to life from initial code to production.

Despite the pressure, I delivered the regression suite well within the allocated time. By the end of the contract, I wasn't just a QA engineer, I was an expert, as I understood every term, nuance, and moving parts of the business. The solid initial stratum of my Agilist-sedimentary rock was formed.

In hindsight, I've learned that a well-run waterfall organization with strong built-in quality practices can sometimes deliver faster than an Agile team that is still finding its rhythm. Agility, after all, is only powerful when it's anchored in built-in quality and focus. Would you agree?

The Leap Into Agile

When the contract was coming to a close, I wasn't sure of my next step. Thankfully, a recruiter called about a small but ambitious company. They were experimenting with something new at the time, something called Agile and were looking for a QA champion to help lead the charge.

I'll admit, I had never heard the words Agile or Scrum before, but the opportunity intrigued me. The pay and benefits were better, too, so I decided to take the leap.

The next thing I knew, I was standing in my very first stand-up meeting, wide-eyed, curious, and excited. I was officially in the world of Agile.
